# 4MAT reference — training handout faithful, research-aware

## Canonical structure (from supplied scanned pages 4–15)

**Mini Šta / Mini What:** state what the audience can expect. This is an orientation preface, not a replacement for Why.

1. **ZAŠTO / WHY** — why should this audience care? Open, engage, connect to shared experience and goals; examples, stories and an authentic metaphor. Attend to their expectations. Reconnect later sections to Why.
2. **ŠTA / WHAT** — what is the central claim, fact, model or idea? Explain and substantiate; move from overview to evidence; invite discussion or peer explanation when feasible.
3. **KAKO / HOW** — how does it work and how can it be used? Demo with steps, activity, or relevant operational walkthrough. Presenter demonstrates only what the explanation has prepared the audience to understand.
4. **ŠTA AKO / WHAT IF (AND IF NOT)** — what happens if adopted / adapted / ignored? Ask reflective questions, consider benefits and limitations, transfer into future, and conclude with a concrete choice or next step.

The printed sequence is explicit on pages 5–7 and 15, and storyboarding on pages 8–10. Spoken narration without slides is equally valid; a static website may need navigation cues rather than speaking cues.

### Suggested 8-step lens (from supplied research synthesis; optional)

| Phase | Step | Audience task | Illustration |
|---|---|---|---|
| WHY | Connect | Recognize stakes | Reality/story |
| WHY | Attend | Reflect on gap | Short audience question |
| WHAT | Image | See mental structure | One-page model |
| WHAT | Inform | Learn claim and evidence | Source-backed explanations |
| HOW | Practice | See/use procedure | Demo, worked example |
| HOW | Extend | Try variant | User choice or practice |
| WHAT IF | Refine | Scrutinize pros/cons | Conditions and risks |
| WHAT IF | Perform | Decide next step | Call to action |

Do **not** present this eight-stage labeling or 'right/left hemisphere modes' as an empirically verified mapping of brain activity.

## Storyboarding workflow

1. Brainstorm source-backed and proposed content as atomic post-its. Preserve the source reference and uncertainty on each item.
2. Chunk from general themes to specific evidence/examples; group by common denominator.
3. Start with the audience **outcome** and plan dependencies backward.
4. Allocate every included chunk to Mini What / WHY / WHAT / HOW / WHAT IF. Estimate times. Explicitly mark nonincluded material as appendix/parking lot.
5. For long workshops, nest short 4MAT units within the overall sequence, and mark each unit with a `cycle` id in `presentation.json`. For a five-minute pitch, compress each phase but do not remove HOW or WHAT IF. Format-specific shapes are in `knowledge/FORMATS.md`.

## Transitions are part of the method

Each phase answers the question the previous phase raised:

- **Mini What → WHY**: "here is what we'll cover" becomes "here is why it matters to *you*".
- **WHY → WHAT**: "so what is it, exactly?"
- **WHAT → HOW**: "how does it work in practice?"
- **HOW → WHAT IF**: "what becomes possible, and what if we don't?"

Write an explicit bridge sentence for each transition, and re-anchor to the WHY after heavy WHAT sections. A deck whose sections could be shuffled without anyone noticing is four presentations, not one.

## Audience / outcomes

Presenter outcome and audience outcome are distinct. Ask: What is required? What do I want to achieve? Who is attending? What do they already know? What assumptions do they bring? What expectations and needs are evidenced? Why would they care? What would count as success? What can be observed afterward?

Do **not** claim everyone has a fixed learning style or 'unlock 100% of the room.' The old handout offers percentages 35%, 22%, 18%, and the research report infers 25% for the fourth quadrant; these are inherited claims, not independently substantiated prevalence estimates. The goal of multiple modalities is to increase opportunities for understanding, not classify people.

## Basic timing defaults (design heuristics, not research facts)

Short talk: 5% Mini What; 25% Why; 30% What; 25% How; 15% What If. These ratios are editable and include any Q&A. Workshops should allocate more time to hands-on How and What If. Do not confuse phase weight with learning-style percentages.

## Proof status vocabulary

- `[SOURCE]` — directly supported by an identifiable document, named section/page/URL.
- `[INTERPRETATION]` — reasoning based on source, not an empirical finding.
- `[PROPOSAL]` — invented or user-designed exercise, metaphor, feature, or step.
- `[HYPOTHESIS]` — testable claim not yet demonstrated.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` — the source itself is weak or unclear, a reference is missing, or a number is unresolved.

**Tense and modality check**: a source's "I could observe", "we plan to" or "imagine a child who…" is a `[PROPOSAL]` or an illustration. It must never become "I observed", "we built" or "children did" in a script. Personal experiences count as `[SOURCE]` only in the words the presenter actually supplied. Any sensory or emotional detail added for effect needs the presenter's explicit approval.

Cite URLs in evidence.md and attach claim IDs to script/slides, especially on numbers, impacts, outcomes and future predictions.
