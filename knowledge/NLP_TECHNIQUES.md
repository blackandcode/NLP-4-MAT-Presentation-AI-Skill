# NLP-inspired techniques: selection guide and evidence status

NLP techniques here are **optional communication tools**. They are not a checklist and they are not proven mechanisms. A strong presentation may use none of them by name. The audience should only hear NLP terminology when the talk is *about* NLP (for example, an NLP Master defense).

## Selection rules

1. Start from the problem that has been diagnosed, never from the technique. Examples: "WHY feels generic", "the audience will be sceptical", "the HOW is abstract", "the speaker is nervous".
2. Choose **0–4 techniques** per presentation. Two well-executed techniques beat eight token ones.
3. For each chosen technique, write one line: *problem → technique → where in the 4MAT flow → how it stays transparent*.
4. Prefer the minimal set below. Add pocket-skill exercises only when the user wants deeper work.
5. Every technique must survive the **transparency test**: would the presenter be comfortable telling the audience what they are doing and why? If not, drop it.

**Minimal default set** (often enough): outcome framing, an opening that establishes common ground, precise questions (Meta Model), one worked HOW example run as a T.O.T.E. loop, a two-sided WHAT IF (towards / away from), and mental rehearsal for the presenter.

## Technique matrix

Evidence status vocabulary: **craft**: a sensible practice with no specific controlled evidence for presentations; **related research**: adjacent scientific findings support the general idea, but not the NLP-specific claim; **unsupported claim**: the mechanism as stated in NLP literature is not supported, so don't assert it.

| Technique | Use when | Skip when | 4MAT placement | Evidence status and safe framing | Deeper skill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Outcome framing** (presenter vs. audience outcome; *"what else?"*) | Always, at the brief | Never | Brief, Mini What, WHAT IF | Related research (goal clarity in planning and instructional design). Treat as planning practice. | `4mat-interview` |
| **Common-ground opening / pacing and leading** (the "yes-set" in the handout) | The audience is mixed, sceptical or arrives with different expectations | The opening already starts with a strong, relevant story | WHY opening | Craft. Use *true, checkable* statements about the room. Never use it to manufacture agreement or lead into a sales suggestion. The covert-command framing in the training handout is not supported and is excluded. | `4mat-nested-loops` |
| **Meta Model questions** ("What specifically?", "Compared to what?", "How will you know?") | Claims are vague; audience objections are vague; designing Q&A | Never harmful when used respectfully | WHAT, HOW, Q&A | Craft (consistent with good clarification and interviewing practice) | `4mat-sleight-of-mouth` |
| **Milton-style open language** (permissive invitations, "imagine…") | Inviting imagination in WHY or WHAT IF | Formal or sceptical audiences that expect precision; whenever precision is needed | WHY, WHAT IF | Craft. Frame it as an invitation, never as hypnosis or unconscious influence. | `4mat-nested-loops` |
| **Metaphor and story** | The idea is abstract, or the audience needs meaning before detail | The story would overshadow the subject, or no genuine story exists | WHY, WHAT (Image) | Related research (narrative and concreteness aid comprehension and memory). Never invent a personal story. | `4mat-nested-loops`, `4mat-logical-levels` |
| **Open story loops** (start a story and finish it later) | Talks of 10 minutes or more that need a narrative thread | Short pitches; when suspense would withhold safety or factual information | Open in WHY, close in WHAT IF | Craft (narrative curiosity). The classic Zeigarnik memory effect has an inconsistent replication record, so don't claim open loops "bypass" judgement or improve memory. | `4mat-nested-loops` |
| **Chunking** (up to purpose, down to examples) | The material jumps between abstraction levels; the audience is lost or bored | — | WHAT structure, transitions | Craft (consistent with general-to-specific instructional sequencing) | `4mat-storyboard` |
| **Perceptual positions** (self / other / observer / system) | The talk is trapped in the speaker's view; designing for empathy or stakeholder analysis | The user wants a quick draft | Research and design; WHY; WHAT IF ecology | Related research (perspective-taking). Second-position insights are **hypotheses** to check with real audience members, not mind-reading. | `4mat-perceptual-positions` |
| **Values, criteria and beliefs; reframing** (Sleight of Mouth patterns) | Predictable objections; a defensive Q&A; limiting beliefs in the material | The objection is a legitimate safety, evidence or ethics concern. Answer it; don't reframe it away. | WHAT, HOW, Q&A | Craft. Reframing must never dismiss a valid concern. | `4mat-sleight-of-mouth` |
| **Towards / away-from framing** (metaprogram) | Designing WHAT IF / WHAT IF NOT | — | WHAT IF | Related research (gain/loss message framing). Present both sides honestly. Typing individuals by metaprogram is not validated. | `4mat-storyboard` |
| **T.O.T.E. loop** (Test–Operate–Test–Exit; Miller, Galanter and Pribram 1960) | Structuring a HOW demonstration or practice as visible iteration | — | HOW | A cognitive-psychology feedback model; using it as a demo structure is craft | `4mat-disney-strategy` |
| **Disney creative strategy** (Dreamer / Realist / Critic) | The vision is either untethered or killed by premature criticism; idea development | The content is already well structured | Design; WHAT, HOW, WHAT IF | Craft (deferring judgement while generating ideas is standard ideation practice). Use roles as thinking modes, not personality types. | `4mat-disney-strategy` |
| **Logical levels** (environment → behaviour → capability → beliefs/values → identity → purpose) | Connecting a concrete demo to meaning; aligning a personal story with a model | Purely technical or decision-focused talks | WHY ↔ WHAT IF alignment | Craft (a reflection scaffold; the hierarchy itself is not empirically validated) | `4mat-logical-levels` |
| **Sensory-specific language** (visual / auditory / kinesthetic predicates) | The script is abstract and flat | — | All phases | Related research (concrete language aids comprehension). The claim that matching a person's "preferred representational system" improves outcomes is **unsupported**, as is "learning styles" matching (Pashler et al. 2008). | `4mat-nested-loops` |
| **Future pacing** (inviting the audience to imagine applying the idea) | WHAT IF transfer; workshops | High-stakes decisions where it could feel manipulative | WHAT IF | Craft. Make it an explicit, optional invitation. | `4mat-nested-loops` |
| **Presenter mental rehearsal** (associated run-through with success evidence) | The presenter is preparing a high-stakes delivery | — | Pre-talk | Related research (mental practice can aid performance). Do not claim it "programs" outcomes. | `4mat-state-anchor` |
| **Presenter state: breathing, posture, resource anchor, wide-angle gaze** | Nerves or a flat delivery | The presenter finds it distracting | Pre-talk, delivery | Craft. Wide-angle gaze helps the presenter scan the room. Claims that it silences internal dialogue or unlocks the unconscious are **unsupported**. | `4mat-state-anchor` |
| **Spatial consistency on stage** (past / present / future positions; a separate spot for objections) | Live stage talks with room to move, especially timelines and contrasts | Lectern-bound, online or recorded talks | Delivery | Related research (left-to-right mental timelines in left-to-right reading cultures; mirror for right-to-left). Persuasive "anchor contamination" effects are **unsupported**. Present it as theatrical clarity. | `4mat-state-anchor` |
| **Calibration** (watching the audience and adjusting) | Every live talk; especially workshops | — | All phases, Q&A | Craft (standard facilitation). Treat what you observe as signals to check, not conclusions about inner states. | `4mat-rehearse` |
| **Ecology check** (consequences for the wider system) | The proposal affects other people: children, users, organisations | — | WHAT IF / WHAT IF NOT | Craft (comparable to stakeholder and risk analysis) | `4mat-perceptual-positions` |
| **Satir categories as gesture vocabulary** | Coaching body language for a stage talk | Most talks; any audience that would notice theatrics | Delivery | In Satir's model, *placating*, *blaming*, *computing* and *distracting* are stress stances and *levelling* is the congruent one. NLP trainers repurpose them as gesture styles. Default to levelling; never use "blamer" energy at people. | `4mat-state-anchor` |

## Hard exclusions

- No covert or embedded commands, no "bypassing conscious resistance", no installing anything in "the unconscious".
- No claims about brain hemispheres, mirror neurons, the reticular activating system or "programming" the audience.
- No classifying audiences into fixed learning styles or into the handout's 35% / 22% / 18% / 25% shares.
- No NLP efficacy claims. Systematic reviews do not support them: Sturt et al. 2012 on health outcomes; Witkowski 2010 on NLP's core claims.

## Content vs. process

Keep two tracks, as in the training handout. **Content** is what is claimed and how well it is supported. **Process** is how the presenter prepares, connects, paces, moves and checks understanding. Record process cues separately from the spoken words (`process_notes`, or bracketed cues in `script.md`).
