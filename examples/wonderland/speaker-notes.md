# Wonderland: From Children's Imagination to Creative Action — speaker notes

Audience: NLP Master trainers and academic examiners | Duration: 15 minutes | Medium: slides+speech

## 01. Wonderland — MINI WHAT / ORIENTATION (0.5 min)

Today I will introduce a question behind my NLP Master project: how can we support children in turning imagination into something they can explore, revise and make their own? I will distinguish the educational approach from its first digital demonstration, Wonderland.

**Process cue:** Open with a concise map of the presentation; do not describe expected effects as proven.

**Visual production direction:** A blank world gradually receiving a child's creations (proposed illustration)

**Evidence IDs:** C001, C002

## 02. Whose imagination leads? — WHY / MEANING (1.5 min)

The origin of this project is a personal educational value: children should have room to ask unusual questions, try alternatives and discover their own ways to build ideas. I am not claiming that one application can guarantee those qualities. I am arguing that the process of supporting a child's authorship is worth designing and evaluating.

**Process cue:** Pause briefly for recognition; don't ask for a public confession.

**Visual production direction:** Two contrasting response paths: 'That won't work' versus 'What do you imagine?'

**Audience prompt:** Think of a time someone helped you develop an idea instead of replacing it.

**Evidence IDs:** C001, C003

## 03. A future worth leaving behind — WHY / MEANING (2 min)

My broader motivation is intergenerational: I want to leave a better world for those who follow us. As an engineer, I translated one version of that ambition into a digital creative environment. But the theory and the child-centered values matter more than the application. It could eventually take many forms beyond software.

**Visual production direction:** From child-led question to potential future benefit; a scenario, not measured causality

**Evidence IDs:** C001, C004

## 04. The strategy is the project — WHAT / UNDERSTANDING (1.5 min)

This project is about modeling creativity-supporting processes using selected NLP concepts, not about proving a technological product. Wonderland is one proposed implementation of those principles. Wonder Creator is an initial tool and Lumi is the owl-shaped guide that asks questions. The child remains the author.

**Visual production direction:** Two-layer model: creative-strategy principles above, Wonderland/Wonder Creator below

**Evidence IDs:** C002, C005

## 05. Three roles, one creative cycle — WHAT / UNDERSTANDING (2 min)

The Disney creative strategy offers an accessible organizing metaphor: Dreamer, Realist and Critic or Analyst. In our proposed child-friendly adaptation, these are not permanent labels. The child can return to any role. Other NLP-inspired tools can improve questions, for example by asking 'What specifically?' when a request is too broad, or by reflecting on the impact on a turtle or another character.

**Visual production direction:** Circular concept diagram with the child's decision at the center

**Evidence IDs:** C005, C006

## 06. An empty world becomes an idea — HOW / APPLICATION (2 min)

The practical demonstration is intentionally simple. We begin with a blank world and ask to create an object. We then request a change. The point is not to showcase AI image quality. It is to show a loop where the child's description and decisions cause visible changes. This shows an interaction concept; it does not establish educational efficacy.

**Process cue:** Prepare an offline fallback; never promise a live capability not verified immediately before presenting.

**Visual production direction:** LIVE DEMO: show the current Wonder Creator if available; otherwise use preapproved screenshots of the working prototype

**Audience prompt:** What one question would help the child decide the next change?

**Evidence IDs:** C002, C007

## 07. Lumi asks, children decide — HOW / APPLICATION (1 min)

Imagine a child proposing a robot that cleans the ocean. Lumi does not design the entire robot. It asks what the child wants it to do, how a first attempt could be checked, and whether it affects a turtle. This is an illustrative conversation from the project document, not an observed result of a user study.

**Visual production direction:** Illustrative dialog, clearly labeled imagined example

**Evidence IDs:** C005, C008

## 08. Try. Check. Improve. — HOW / APPLICATION (1.5 min)

The T.O.T.E. sequence means Test, Operate, Test, Exit. For example, a bridge may fail to reach both towers. Rather than dismissing the attempt, Lumi can ask what difference the child sees. The child may decide to make the bridge longer. The loop is useful as a design scaffold, but whether using it improves transferable creativity remains to be tested.

**Visual production direction:** Four-step loop with an example: extend a bridge until it connects two towers

**Evidence IDs:** C006, C009

## 09. What if we expand the canvas? — WHAT IF / FUTURE (1.5 min)

What if we gradually moved from voice-guided drawings to three-dimensional objects or shared creative worlds? Those are future plans, not current working capabilities. We must also ask what could go wrong: a polished AI-generated result may conceal that the child did little thinking. Product decisions would need careful attention to consent, accessibility and privacy.

**Visual production direction:** Roadmap layers clearly separated into current demo, next experiment and long-term vision

**Evidence IDs:** C007, C010

## 10. Vision is not evidence—yet — WHAT IF / FUTURE (1.5 min)

The existing prototype demonstrates that a digital object can be created or changed through an interaction. It does not show that children's creativity has improved. A sensible next step would be testing usability with adults first, then considering a child-centered pilot with parental consent and appropriate safeguarding. We should assess the child's ideas, reasons and revisions, not only the generated picture. I invite feedback on what should be measured first and what would make this approach worth developing further.

**Process cue:** End with the honest limitation and one concrete invitation; do not end on an unsupported success claim.

**Visual production direction:** Evaluation ladder: feasibility → pilot → independent assessment → long-term validation

**Audience prompt:** Which one observable indicator would make a pilot genuinely informative?

**Evidence IDs:** C010, C011
