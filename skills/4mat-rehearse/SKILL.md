# Rehearsal and defense loop

Use the approved script or deck and the target format. Start by summarising one-line cues per phase: preview, relevance, core idea, demonstration, future and next step. Then offer a mode:

- **(A)** a timed full run
- **(B)** hard Q&A from this specific audience
- **(C)** opening and closing only
- **(D)** a live-demo failure drill
- **(E)** transitions only

**Timing honesty.** In a text-only environment, don't claim to have measured spoken performance. Estimate at the stated rate (default 120–150 words per minute; adjust for language and register). Add demo, activity and pause time separately, and compare the total with the slot.

**Q&A simulation.**
- Derive questions from `evidence.md`, especially "Cannot claim yet", the gaps and the counter-arguments, and from the audience lens in `knowledge/FORMATS.md` (resolve under `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/`, then `.4mat-studio/`, then the repository root). Don't use generic questions.
- Ask one question at a time and wait for the answer. Give respectful, evidence-grounded feedback on accuracy, brevity, honesty about limits and composure, then let the presenter try again.
- Typical patterns:
  - *Academic*: "What exactly is modeled vs. illustrated?", "What can the prototype do today?", "What would count as evidence?", "Why this framework rather than an established alternative?", "How are participants protected?"
  - *Investor*: "What's measured vs. projected?", "Why now? Why you?", "What would kill this?", "What exactly do you need, and for which milestone?"
  - *Educator or parent*: "How much time does this take?", "What about safety and data?", "What if a learner doesn't respond?"

**Delivery support.** Optionally offer `4mat-state-anchor` routines (rehearsal, breathing, scanning the room). Recommend honest audience check-ins.

**Ending.** Finish with concrete practice priorities and any unresolved claims. No hollow cheerleading.
