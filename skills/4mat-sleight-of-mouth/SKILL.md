# 4MAT Sleight of Mouth & Objection Reframing (`4mat-sleight-of-mouth`)

This pocket skill applies Robert Dilts' **Sleight of Mouth** belief-change patterns and NLP **Context/Content Reframing** to presentation scripts, demonstrations, and high-stakes Q&A defenses (`4MAT_OCR_kompletno.md`, pp. 6, 10, 14; `Wonderland_NLP_Master - Verzija 1.2.md`, §§3.4, 4.3).

**Target Failure Mode:** Sounding defensive when challenged, overclaiming to hide limitations, or letting audience skepticism contaminate the speaker's central stage anchor.

## 1. Core Presentation Reframe Patterns

Select the 3–5 patterns that best fit the audience's unspoken objections and the learners' internal blocks:

1. **Redefine (Shift the Label):**
   - *Objection/Belief:* "Is this just another AI app / startup pitch for kids?"
   - *Reframe:* "This isn't a startup pitch or a replacement for real-world play; it is a long-term approach to modeling and supporting creative thinking—whether in a parent-child workshop (*Kreativni geni*), a physical maker space, or a digital sketchpad."
2. **Intention (Honor the Positive Protective Motive):**
   - *Objection/Belief:* "Screen tools and AI can make children passive consumers instead of thinkers."
   - *Reframe:* "The intention behind that concern—protecting the child as the active author of their own imagination—is the exact design rule of this project: Lumi never draws ten unsolicited objects; she asks a question so the child makes the creative decision."
3. **Chunk Down (Meta-Model Specificity against 'Ne mogu'):**
   - *Objection/Belief:* "I can never draw an animal / We can't prove long-term creativity transfer yet."
   - *Reframe:* "Instead of trying to solve everything at once, ask: *'Which specific part is tricky right now?'* or *'What is the smallest, safest consent-based pilot we can run first to observe whether guided questions help?'*"
4. **Counterexample / Feedback Frame:**
   - *Objection/Belief:* "The wings failed / The bridge didn't reach the second tower."
   - *Reframe:* *"Sada znamo koji dio krila treba promijeniti."* An incomplete first version is not a verdict on ability; it is the informative comparison step inside a **T.O.T.E.** loop.
5. **Hierarchy of Criteria (Appeal to the Highest Shared Value):**
   - *Objection/Belief:* "A child's drawing or prototype is simple compared to commercial software."
   - *Reframe:* "More important than how polished the image looks on screen is whether the child leaves with the lived belief: *'I can imagine, try, notice what to change, and build my own world.'*"
6. **Meta-Frame / Long-Term Learning Horizon:**
   - *Objection/Belief:* "Your Master thesis hasn't tested 100 children yet."
   - *Reframe:* "A Master thesis that honestly separates its working demonstrator from future empirical testing is far more trustworthy than premature claims—because this work is a marathon and a new beginning (*novi početak*), not a finished sprint."

## 2. Stage Choreography for Objections (The Isolated Critic Spot)

When pre-framing or answering a tough objection live:
1. **Step Off-Center** to the **Isolated Critic Spot** (or gesture toward an empty space off to the side) when voicing the harsh objection or limiting belief.
2. **Pause and Acknowledge** the positive intention behind the question (*Placater → Leveller*).
3. **Step Back into Center Stage** (or Audience-Right for future possibilities) before delivering the Sleight of Mouth reframe and evidence-backed answer.

## 3. Output Contract

Generate an **Objection & Sleight of Mouth Matrix** (for `nlp-pocket-analysis.md` and `qa.md`) listing the top 5 audience objections or learner limiting beliefs, the named Sleight of Mouth pattern used, the exact spoken reframe, the evidence ID (`C001`–`C015`), and the stage/vocal cue.
